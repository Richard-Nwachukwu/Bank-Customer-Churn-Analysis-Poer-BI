# Bank-Customer-Churn-Analysis-Power-BI

## Problem Statement

Bank customer churn is a critical challenge, impacting revenue and long-term growth. This project aims to identify the factors influencing customer churn, derive actionable insights from the data, and propose strategies to retain valuable customers. By analyzing the behavior and demographics of customers who leave the bank, we can develop loyalty programs and retention campaigns to minimize churn and maximize customer satisfaction.

## Objective

The primary objective is to analyze customer churn and identify key factors influencing attrition. Insights derived from this analysis will support banks in:

1. Designing effective loyalty programs.

2. Implementing proactive retention strategies.

3. Enhancing customer engagement and satisfaction.

## Deliverables

1. A detailed report with insights on customer churn factors.

2. Visualizations highlighting key trends and patterns.

3. Recommendations for churn prevention and customer retention.

## Key Insights

### 1. Customer Overview

Total Customers: 10,000

Active Customers: 5,151 (51.51%)

Inactive Customers: 4,849 (48.49%)

Retained Customers: 7,963 (79.63%)

Exited Customers: 2,037 (20.37%)

### 2. Demographics and Churn

Gender

Female Churn Rate: 44.08% (898 customers)

Male Churn Rate: 55.92% (1,139 customers)

Recommendation: Investigate reasons behind higher male churn.

Geography

Germany: Highest churn rate (0.34) and most exit customers (814).

France: Churn rate (0.162), Exit Customers: 810.

Spain: Churn rate (0.167), Exit Customers: 413.

Recommendation: Focus retention campaigns on high-churn locations like Germany.

### 3. Age and Tenure

Mid-Career and Mature Professionals contribute the highest churn (703 and 702 customers respectively).

Young Adults (40) and Seniors (1) have minimal churn.

Recommendation: Develop loyalty programs for mid-career professionals.

### 4. Credit and Financial Metrics

Customers with Poor and Fair Credit Scores contribute the most to churn (520 and 685 exits).

High-Income Customers show the highest churn (527), followed by Upper-Middle Income (517).

Recommendation: Offer financial planning services to high-income customers.

### 5. Customer Activity

Active Members: Less likely to churn (51.51% active).

Inactive Members: Contribute significantly to churn (48.49% inactive).

Recommendation: Engage inactive members with personalized campaigns.

### 6. Credit Card Holding

Credit Card Holders: 30.09% churn rate (613 exits).

Non-Credit Card Holders: 69.91% churn rate (1,424 exits).

Recommendation: Encourage non-credit card holders to enroll for credit cards to improve retention.

### Seasonal Trends

Churn Rate by Month: Spikes in November (highest) and dips in February.

Recommendation: Launch seasonal retention campaigns in Q4.

## Recommendations

### 1. Retention Strategies

Geography: Focus on Germany and France with targeted retention offers.

Customer Segments:

Design specific programs for mid-career and mature professionals.

Develop tailored campaigns for high-income customers.

Financial Products:

Promote credit card offerings to non-holders.

Provide incentives to customers with low credit scores and balances.

### 2. Customer Engagement

Increase interactions with inactive members through personalized engagement strategies.

Launch educational programs to improve credit scores and financial well-being.

### 3. Loyalty Programs

Offer discounts or rewards for long-tenure customers.

Create tiered loyalty benefits to incentivize continued banking with the institution.

### 4. Seasonal Campaigns

Address churn spikes during November with proactive customer outreach.

Deploy financial wellness campaigns early in the year to build trust.

## Conclusion

By addressing the identified churn drivers, the bank can improve retention rates and foster stronger customer relationships. Focusing on high-risk segments such as inactive members, customers in Germany, and mid-career professionals, while enhancing engagement for high-income customers, will yield significant gains. Implementing these strategies will reduce churn and support sustained business growth.
